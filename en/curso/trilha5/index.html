✔ Customer service checklist with AI

A step-by-step guide to using AI before, during, and after every professional appointment.

0 of 6 0%
What it is:

A standardized process that ensures you use AI consistently across all client interactions, without missing important steps.

Why learn:

Without a script, you use AI when you remember and waste time reinventing the process. The checklist turns occasional use into a productive habit.

Key concepts:

Process standardization, consistent quality, fewer errors of omission, cumulative gains.

What it is:

Use AI to review the client’s history, prepare key questions, research relevant updates, and anticipate needs before the meeting.

Why learn:

Arriving prepared for a client meeting is what separates exceptional professionals from average ones. AI does in 5 minutes what would take 30.

Key concepts:

Pre-service briefing, background research, smart questions, anticipating objections.

What it is:

Techniques for using AI as support during a conversation with a client: quick lookups, automatic note-taking, and real-time fact-checking.

Why learn:

Having AI as backup increases your confidence and response speed without the client noticing.

Key concepts:

Discreet consultation, second screen, data validation, real-time recording of key points.

What it is:

Automate post-meeting tasks: generate a meeting summary, draft a follow-up email, update client records, and define next steps.

Why learn:

Follow-up is where most professionals fall short. With AI, you can do it in 5 minutes and never forget a single point.

Key concepts:

Automatic summary, follow-up email, CRM/medical record update, documented next steps.

What it is:

How to adapt the generic checklist to the realities of your profession: items specific to your field, level of detail, and integration with your tools.

Why learn:

A generic checklist helps, but a customized one makes a difference. You create the process that works for YOUR type of practice.

Key concepts:

Customization by profession, reusable templates, process iteration, reduced friction.

What it is:

Simple metrics to measure AI’s real impact on your client service: time saved, perceived quality, client satisfaction, and number of follow-ups.

Why learn:

Without measuring, you don’t know whether AI is helping or getting in the way. Concrete numbers motivate you to keep going and improve.

Key concepts:

Personal benchmark, time per task, output quality, rework rate, customer satisfaction.

📅 Weekly AI use routine

A day-by-day plan to integrate AI into your workweek without overloading your schedule.

0 of 6 0%
What it is:

Use AI at the start of the week to review commitments, prioritize tasks, and prepare briefs for the week’s appointments.

Why learn:

Starting the week organized boosts your productivity over the other 4 days. AI does the planning in 15 minutes.

Key concepts:

Weekly review, AI-powered prioritization, agenda preparation, client briefing.

What it is:

Set aside Tuesday and Wednesday to use AI to write emails, proposals, posts, and communications materials for the office.

Why learn:

Focusing your writing output helps you scale. Instead of 1 post a week, you create 3 with the same effort.

Key concepts:

Batch communication, AI-assisted drafts, human review, authority building.

What it is:

Set aside Thursday for tasks that require more thought: data analysis, preparing business proposals, and business strategy.

Why learn:

Well-crafted proposals generate revenue. AI speeds up research and drafting, while you focus on strategic judgment.

Key concepts:

AI-assisted analysis, AI-generated sales proposal, market research, strategic pricing.

What it is:

End the week by evaluating what worked, adjusting prompts and processes, and preparing improvements for the next week.

Why learn:

Without review, you repeat the same mistakes. The weekly retrospective is what turns basic use into mastery.

Key concepts:

Weekly retrospective, prompt adjustments, personal prompt library, incremental improvement.

What it is:

The ideal amount of time to spend on AI each day: 30 minutes is enough to build a habit and get results without overloading your schedule.

Why learn:

Less than 15 minutes has no impact; more than 1 hour competes with productive work. The sweet spot is 30 focused minutes.

Key concepts:

Dedicated time block, daily habit, ROI on time invested, consistency over intensity.

What it is:

A month-by-month roadmap showing how your AI skills progress: month 1, basic tasks; month 2, combined workflows; month 3, automation and full integration.

Why learn:

Knowing where you are on the learning curve reduces anxiety and shows that progress is normal and predictable.

Key concepts:

Learning curve, progress milestones, digital maturity, from user to fluent.

🚀 From basics to advanced — next steps

Your action plan for continuing to grow with AI after the course. Self-assessment, community, and the next 30 days.

0 of 6 0%
What it is:

A quick assessment of your current AI proficiency: which skills you’ve already mastered, which you need to strengthen, and where your greatest potential for improvement lies.

Why learn:

Knowing where you are is the first step to knowing where to go. It helps you avoid wasting time repeating what you already know.

Key concepts:

Competency matrix, self-assessment, skill gaps, learning prioritization.

What it is:

No-code tools like Zapier, Make, and built-in shortcuts connect AI to your email, calendar, and CRM without requiring you to code.

Why learn:

Automating repetitive tasks eliminates the need to remember to use AI. It runs on its own in the background.

Key concepts:

No-code automation, triggers and actions, tool integration, automated workflows.

What it is:

Reliable sources, newsletters, and channels to keep up with AI news without spending hours scrolling through social media.

Why learn:

AI improves every week. Those who stop learning lose their competitive edge within a few months.

Key concepts:

Source curation, just-in-time learning, controlled experimentation, information diet.

What it is:

Groups, forums, and communities where independent professionals share prompts, workflows, and real results with AI.

Why learn:

Learning on your own is slow. An active community shortens the learning curve and brings ideas you would never come up with on your own.

Key concepts:

Peer learning, prompt sharing, benchmarking among peers, networking around AI.

What it is:

Advanced AI applications for professionals who have already mastered the basics: agents, API integration, and creating custom tools for the office.

Why learn:

Going beyond the basics creates competitive barriers. Your firm starts operating at a level that competitors can’t easily replicate.

Key concepts:

AI agents, custom GPTs, API integration, sustainable competitive advantage.

What it is:

A concrete week-by-week plan for the next 30 days, with simple, measurable goals to reinforce what you learned in the course.

Why learn:

Courses without an action plan become memories. With a 30-day roadmap, you turn knowledge into a lasting habit.

Key concepts:

30-day plan, SMART goals, gradual habit-building, personal accountability.
