Monday: weekly planning and organization

Monday is the day for overview. Instead of diving into operations, spend 30 minutes with AI mapping out the entire week: commitments, deadlines, priorities, and what can be delegated or automated.

📋 Prompt: Monday planning

Help me plan my week as an independent professional: MY PROFESSION: [your field] COMMITMENTS THIS WEEK: - [list meetings, appointments, deadlines] OUTSTANDING ITEMS FROM LAST WEEK: - [list what remains open] GENERATE: 1. Priorities for the week (3 main goals) 2. Task distribution by day (Mon to Fri) 3. What I can delegate or automate with AI 4. Suggested time blocks for each task 5. A motivational reminder for the week

💡 Tip: 30-minute ritual

Block 30 minutes on your calendar every Monday at 8 a.m. Coffee + AI + planning. In 4 weeks, you won’t be able to start the week without it. The secret is never to skip it.

Tuesday-Wednesday: communication and content production

Tuesday and Wednesday are days for proactive communication. It's when you create content for social media, respond to clients you've left waiting, write proposals, and build your professional presence.

1

Tuesday morning: pending replies

Open your email/WhatsApp inbox. Use the customer response prompt (Track 4) for each pending message. Goal: clear all pending messages by lunch.

2

Tuesday afternoon: this week's content

Use the authority-building content prompt (Track 4). Produce 2-3 posts for the entire week at once.

3

Wednesday: proposals and follow-ups

Draft business proposals (Track 4) and send follow-ups for recent appointments (Track 5.1).

💬 Prompt: weekly content batch

Create 3 posts for this week for my professional network: PROFESSION: [your field] AUDIENCE: [clients / colleagues / both] NETWORK: [LinkedIn / Instagram / both] THEME OF THE WEEK: [choose 1 theme from your day-to-day professional life] GENERATE FOR EACH POST: - Title/hook (the first sentence that grabs attention) - Body (150-200 words) - CTA (call to action) - 5 relevant hashtags - Best time to post FORMAT: plain text, no excessive emojis, professional yet approachable tone.

Thursday: analysis and business proposals

On Thursday, the focus is strategic. It's the day to look at the numbers, analyze how the week is going, and prepare proposals or documents that require more thought.

📊 Prompt: weekly review (Thursday)

Analyze how my week is going: PROFESSION: [your field] WEEKLY GOALS (set on Monday): 1. [goal 1 - status: done/pending/in progress] 2. [goal 2 - status] 3. [goal 3 - status] APPOINTMENTS COMPLETED: [number] PROPOSALS SENT: [number] CONTENT PUBLISHED: [number of posts] GENERATE: 1. Diagnosis: am I on track or behind? 2. What to prioritize tomorrow (Friday) to wrap up the week well 3. Suggested adjustments for next week 4. An insight about productivity based on the data

📑 Thursday checklist

  • ▢ Review goals: of Monday’s 3 goals are on track?
  • ▢ Prepare a proposal: if there's a pending opportunity, develop it using the Trilha 4 template
  • ▢ Financial analysis: fees received vs. projected for the month
  • ▢ Set priorities: what’s the single most important thing for Friday?

💰 Prompt: monthly financial forecast

Help me organize my financial forecast: PROFESSION: [your field] CURRENT MONTH: [month/year] FEES RECEIVED SO FAR: R$ [amount] FEES TO BE RECEIVED (confirmed): R$ [amount] PENDING PROPOSALS: [number] totaling R$ [amount] FIXED MONTHLY EXPENSES: R$ [amount] GENERATE: 1. Monthly revenue forecast (optimistic/realistic/pessimistic scenario) 2. Gap between expenses and projected revenue 3. Suggestions for closing the gap (if any) 4. Proposal target for next week

Friday: review, learning, and adjustments

Friday is the day for retrospective. What worked? What didn't? Which prompts produced good results? What should you adjust for next week?

🔍 Prompt: Friday retrospective

Review my professional week: WHAT I DID THIS WEEK: - Appointments: [number] - Proposals sent: [number] - Content published: [number] - Follow-ups completed: [number] WHAT WENT WELL: [describe in 2-3 lines] WHAT DIDN’T GO WELL: [describe in 2-3 lines] GENERATE: 1. Weekly score (0-10) with justification in 1 line 2. Top 3 wins of the week 3. Top 2 adjustments for next week 4. 1 new habit to try next week 5. High-level plan for Monday

Retrospective questions

  • ✓ Which prompts worked best?
  • ✓ How much time did I save with AI?
  • ✓ Did a client praise something specific?
  • ✓ What did I do manually that AI could have done?

Common mistakes in the retrospective

  • ✗ Skip the retrospective because of time constraints
  • ✗ Focus only on what went wrong
  • ✗ Not recording what you learn
  • ✗ Change everything at once the following week

💡 Tip: prompt notebook

Keep a document called "My Prompts" where you save the prompts that got the best results. On Friday, review the notebook and add the new ones. In 1 month, you’ll have a personalized library.

How much time to invest: the 30-minutes-a-day rule

The most common question: "how much time per day should I spend on AI?" The answer: 30 minutes. No more, no less. Enough time to build a habit, and short enough to fit into any schedule.

📊 The math of 30 minutes

  • 30 min/day x 5 days = 2h30/week of AI practice
  • 2h30/week x 4 weeks = 10h/month investment
  • Average feedback: 5-8 hours saved per week after the 2nd month
  • ROI: invest 2h30, save 5-8h = 2-3x return
1

Week 1–2: learning

Almost all of the 30 minutes go toward learning how to use the tool. That’s normal. You may even be slower than without AI during this stage.

2

Week 3–4: productivity balances out

You already have ready-to-use prompts and are starting to save time. Investment and return are at break-even.

3

Month 2 onward: positive return

AI is already saving more time than you invest. Your prompts are finely tuned, and the routine feels natural.

⚠️ Careful: don’t try to do everything on day 1

The biggest mistake is trying to use AI for everything at once. Start with 1 task per day. In the first week, plan only for Monday. In the second week, add communication. In the third, analysis. Build the habit in layers.

Progress month by month: from beginner to fluent in AI

AI fluency doesn’t happen in a day. There is a predictable learning curve that every independent professional goes through. Knowing which stage you're at helps you stay motivated.

M1

Month 1: Explorer

Tests prompts, makes plenty of mistakes, and discovers what works. Feels slower than doing it manually. Normal.

M2

Month 2: Practitioner

You already have favorite prompts saved. Start saving real time. Notice new opportunities you hadn't seen before.

M3

Month 3: Operator

AI is already part of your routine without you having to think about it. Colleagues notice the difference in the quality and speed of your work.

M6

Month 6: Fluent

You teach other professionals. You create your own prompts. AI feels as natural as using your phone. A well-established competitive advantage.

📈 Prompt: monthly AI self-assessment

Assess my AI use this month: PROFESSION: [your field] MONTH: [month/year] TIME USING AI: [X months] WHAT I USED AI FOR THIS MONTH: - [list the tasks you used AI for] WHAT I STILL DO MANUALLY: - [list the tasks you don’t use AI for] DIFFICULTIES: - [what is holding you back or frustrating you] GENERATE: 1. What stage am I at (explorer/practitioner/operator/fluent)? 2. My main achievement this month 3. The next skill to develop 4. A challenge for next month 5. Prediction: when will I reach the next level?

💡 Tip: strategic patience

No one becomes fluent in English in 1 month. It's the same with AI. The difference: the return on investment in AI is much faster. In 3 months, you're already in the black. In 6, you're unbeatable in your field.
