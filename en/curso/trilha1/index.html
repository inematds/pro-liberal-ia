🎯 What AI can (and can't) do for you

Find out where AI adds value to your profession and where your judgment remains irreplaceable.

What it is:

Concrete examples of independent professionals already using AI in their daily work: lawyers reviewing contracts, doctors triaging symptoms, architects generating initial briefs.

Why learn:

You see that AI is already being used by professionals like you, and understand where to start.

Key concepts:

Automating repetitive tasks, writing assistant, information triage, draft generation.

What it is:

AI’s real capabilities: pattern recognition, text generation and review, summarizing long documents, and analyzing structured data.

Why learn:

Knowing where AI excels helps you avoid frustration when asking it to do something outside its scope and maximizes the return on your time.

Key concepts:

Natural language processing, information synthesis, consistency in production, execution speed.

Why learn:

Understanding the limits protects you and your clients. AI is a tool, not a substitute.

Key concepts:

Professional responsibility, mandatory human oversight, algorithmic bias, decisions that require context.

🧩 How AI thinks — the bare minimum

Understand the basic mechanics without becoming a technical expert. Tokens, prompts, and why AI sometimes makes things up.

0 of 6 0%
What it is:

AI doesn’t read words the way we do. It splits everything into chunks called tokens and has a limited context window.

Why learn:

Understanding tokens and the context window explains why AI "forgets" things in long conversations.

Key concepts:

Token (a piece of a word), context window (short-term memory), input/output limit.

What it is:

You can ask AI to write like a formal lawyer, a compassionate doctor, or a direct consultant. It adapts.

Why learn:

Setting the right tone helps AI produce writing that sounds like you, not generic.

Key concepts:

Persona, tone of voice, language register, audience fit.

What it is:

A prompt is the text you type for the AI. The clearer and more specific it is, the better the result.

Why learn:

The quality of the result depends directly on the quality of your instructions.

Key concepts:

Prompt (instruction), context, specificity, examples within the prompt.

What it is:

The first result is rarely perfect. Iterating means asking for adjustments until you get what you need.

Why learn:

Professionals who iterate get 3-5x better results than those who accept the first response.

Key concepts:

Iteration (refinement), feedback for AI, gradual adjustment, convergence of results.

What it is:

A hallucination is when AI generates false information that looks convincing. It cites nonexistent laws and makes up data.

Why learn:

For an independent professional, using false information can have serious consequences. Knowing how to identify it is essential.

Key concepts:

Hallucination (fabrication), cross-checking, primary sources, calibrated confidence.

What it is:

Practical rules about what you can and shouldn't share with AI, taking professional confidentiality into account.

Why learn:

Protecting client data is an ethical and legal obligation. You need to know the limits before you start using it.

Key concepts:

Professional confidentiality, LGPD, anonymization, sensitive data, platform retention policies.

🔮 Choosing your tool — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

Practical comparison of the 3 leading AIs and a recommendation on where to start.

0 of 6 0%
What it is:

OpenAI’s ChatGPT is the best-known AI. It has a simple interface, a large community, plugins, and custom GPTs.

Why learn:

As the most popular tool, you’ll find more tutorials, examples, and colleagues who already use it.

Key concepts:

GPT-4, Free vs. Plus plan, custom GPTs, web interface, and mobile app.

What it is:

Anthropic’s Claude stands out for long texts, document analysis, and accuracy. Ideal for professionals who work with contracts and legal opinions.

Why learn:

If your work involves long documents and careful analysis, Claude may be the best choice.

Key concepts:

Large context window, Projects, document analysis, lower hallucination rate.

What it is:

Google’s Gemini integrates with Gmail, Docs, and Drive. Ideal for anyone who already lives in the Google ecosystem.

Why learn:

If you use Google Workspace at the office, Gemini fits naturally into your workflow.

Key concepts:

Google integration, multimodal (text + image), Gemini Advanced, real-time research.

What it is:

Comparison table of ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini using criteria that matter to independent professionals.

Why learn:

You don't need to test everything. A direct comparison saves time and guides your choice.

Key concepts:

Text quality, speed, price, integration, context length, and each one’s specialties.

What it is:

Practical cost-benefit analysis: what you really get by paying R$100-150/month for a premium plan.

Why learn:

For a professional who charges R$300+/hour, saving 2 hours a week already pays for the plan.

Key concepts:

Paid plan ROI, free plan limits, exclusive features, time-saving calculator.

What it is:

Direct recommendation on which tool to start with, based on your professional profile and needs.

Why learn:

Stop comparing and start using. The best tool is the one you actually use.

Key concepts:

Start with the free version, test it for 2 weeks, switch if needed, and don’t commit to any one tool.
