📞 Customer Service and Communication Prompts (#1-#4)

Four ready-to-use prompts for client meeting preparation, emails, professional WhatsApp messages, and meeting minutes.

0% 0 of 6
What it is:

A prompt that takes case details and generates a summary, outstanding items, key questions, and suggested next steps. Works for consultations, meetings, and hearings.

Why learn:

A prepared professional inspires confidence. AI summarizes in seconds what would take you 15 minutes to reread in patient records and notes.

Key concepts:

Pre-service briefing. History as input. AI-suggested questions. Automated follow-up.

What it is:

Prompt template for generating professional emails in any tone and for any situation: reply, follow-up, proposal, payment reminder, thank-you.

Why learn:

Emails consume 20-30% of a solo professional’s day. With this prompt, you describe what you want to say, and AI generates the finished text in your style.

Key concepts:

Parameters: recipient, subject, tone, objective. Output with greeting, body, CTA, and sign-off. Word limit.

What it is:

Prompt optimized for short WhatsApp messages: confirmations, reminders, quick replies, and informal announcements that maintain professionalism.

Why learn:

WhatsApp is the channel clients use most in Brazil. Poorly written messages come across as unprofessional. Well-written messages convey attentiveness and care.

Key concepts:

Approachable but professional tone. Maximum of 3-4 lines. Moderate emoji use. Clear CTA at the end.

What it is:

Prompt that turns rough meeting notes into structured minutes with: attendees, agenda, decisions, next steps, and owners.

Why learn:

Well-written meeting minutes prevent misunderstandings and serve as a legal record. AI can turn even a transcribed recording into professional minutes in seconds.

Key concepts:

Structure: header, participants, agenda, decisions, follow-ups. Formal format. Identification of action items.

What it is:

Techniques for adapting the 4 generic prompts to the specific realities of your profession: terminology, formats, constraints, and appropriate tone.

Why learn:

A generic prompt works. A prompt tailored to your field works 3x better. The difference is in the details: jargon, format, and ethical constraints.

Key concepts:

Variable substitution. Adding field-specific constraints. Examples for law, healthcare, accounting, and engineering.

What it is:

A hands-on exercise where you apply the 4 prompts in sequence to a real case from your practice: prepare for the appointment, draft the email, send the message, and write the minutes.

Why learn:

Using the prompts together shows how they connect in a real workflow. They aren’t isolated tools; they’re parts of a system.

Key concepts:

Complete practical case. Prompt sequence. Result evaluation. Post-exercise adjustments.

📝 Production Prompts (#5-#7)

Three prompts for drafting documents, analyzing data, and turning notes into professional content.

0% 0 of 6
What it is:

Prompt that turns rough notes into a structured draft of any professional document with standard sections for your field.

Why learn:

It eliminates the blank page. AI generates 80% of the draft, and you refine the 20% that requires professional judgment.

Key concepts:

Raw data as input. Standard format as instructions. [COMPLETE] markers for sections that need human review.

What it is:

Prompt for analyzing numerical data (financial, clinical, performance) and generating actionable insights in accessible language.

Why learn:

Raw data doesn’t communicate. This prompt turns spreadsheets and numbers into narratives you can use in meetings and reports.

Key concepts:

Tabular data as input. Trend analysis. Comparisons. Recommendations based on the numbers.

What it is:

Prompt that takes a finished text and reviews its clarity, tone, and errors, and suggests improvements without losing your authorial voice.

Why learn:

Even good writing can be improved. AI identifies redundancies, softens an aggressive tone, and suggests restructuring that you can accept or reject.

Key concepts:

Parameterized review (focus on clarity, tone, length). Simulated track changes. Preserve the author's voice.

What it is:

How to adjust production prompts to the specifics of your field: legal formatting, medical language, accounting standards.

Why learn:

A medical report differs from a legal opinion. Prompts are the foundation; adapting them is what makes them truly effective.

Key concepts:

Formats by profession. Technical standards as constraints. Practical adaptation examples.

What it is:

Prompt chaining technique: the output of the analysis prompt becomes the input for the drafting prompt, which then becomes the input for the review prompt.

Why learn:

Chained prompts produce better results than a single long prompt. Each step refines the previous one.

Key concepts:

Prompt pipeline. Output as input. Iterative refinement. When to use a chain vs. a single prompt.

What it is:

Hands-on exercise: take real notes from a case, apply the 3 prompts in sequence, and produce a complete professional document.

Why learn:

Practice reinforces learning. By the end of this exercise, you’ll have a real document ready to use.

Key concepts:

End-to-end practical case. Quality assessment. Time invested vs. time saved. Final refinements.

📊 Strategy Prompts (#8-#10)

Three prompts for planning, authoritative content, and analyzing opportunities in your market.

0% 0 of 6
What it is:

Prompt that analyzes your current situation (revenue, clients, time) and generates a 90-day plan with goals, actions, and success metrics.

Why learn:

Independent professionals are always putting out fires. This prompt forces a strategic pause and generates an actionable roadmap.

Key concepts:

Situational analysis. SMART goals. 90-day plan. KPIs by area. Monthly review.

What it is:

Prompt for generating content that positions you as an authority: educational posts, articles, video scripts, and newsletters.

Why learn:

Professional authority attracts better clients. AI creates the draft; you validate it with your expertise and publish it.

Key concepts:

Educational vs. promotional content. Expert tone. Editorial calendar. Platform-specific adaptation.

What it is:

Prompt for analyzing your field's market: competitors, trends, opportunities, and threats. Works as an AI-assisted mini-SWOT analysis.

Why learn:

Understand the market and what sets apart professionals who grow from those who stagnate. AI organizes information you already have into a structured analysis.

Key concepts:

SWOT analysis. Benchmarking. Industry trends. Niche identification. Differentiated positioning.

What it is:

Adjustments to strategic prompts based on your business stage: just starting out, growing, or established.

Why learn:

A professional with 2 years of experience has different needs from someone with 20 years. The prompts need to reflect that.

Key concepts:

Business stage. Priorities by phase. Relevant metrics. Level of ambition.

What it is:

An integrated view of how the 10 prompts connect in day-to-day work: client service feeds production, which feeds strategy, which refines client service.

Why learn:

Standalone prompts help. Integrated prompts transform. A systemic perspective is what separates casual use from professional use.

Key concepts:

Client service-production-strategy cycle. Feedback loop. Prompt evolution through practice.

What it is:

Final exercise: use the 3 strategic prompts to generate a 30-day plan for your business, with goals, content, and market analysis.

Why learn:

Leave this learning path with a concrete plan in hand. This isn't theory; it's a document you'll put into action over the next 4 weeks.

Key concepts:

Action plan. Weekly goals. Scheduled content. Review at the end of the month.

How to use this learning path

1

Read the prompt

Each topic presents a complete prompt with detailed instructions. Read it to understand the structure and purpose.

2

Copy and adapt

Copy the prompt and replace the fields in [brackets] with your real information. The fields are self-explanatory.

3

Use and refine

Paste it into your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini). Evaluate the result. Adjust the prompt as needed. Save your version.

⚡ Tip for getting the most out of it

Don't try to use all 10 prompts at once. Start with the 2 that address your biggest pain points today. In most cases, prompts #1 (pre-service) and #2 (email) deliver results fastest. Master those two, then move on to the others.

Each prompt also works as a starting point. As you use it, you’ll notice adjustments that make sense for your practice. Save your customized versions in a separate document so you don’t lose the improvements you make over time.

By the end of the 3 weeks of practice, you will have a personal library of prompts tailored to your profession. This library is the most valuable asset you build in this course: it stays with you regardless of which AI tool you use in the future.
